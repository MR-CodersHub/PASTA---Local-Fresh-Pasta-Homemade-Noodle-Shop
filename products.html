Italian Culinary Science

The Art of The Perfect Pairing

Every pasta shape has a purpose. Shape thickness, surface ridges, and hollows dictate which sauces cling and which slip away.

Pasta Category Recommended Shapes Sauce Chemistry Best House Match Chef’s Serving Secret
Long & Ribbon Tagliatelle Pappardelle Wide flat surfaces cradle dense sauces with braised meat proteins and emulsion fats. Slow Chianti Beef Ragù Toss with 2 tbsp starchy pasta water and a knob of cold butter off the flame.
Long & Rounded Bronze Spaghetti Bucatini Smooth swirling strands distribute silky oil, light emulsified tomato, and egg emulsions. San Marzano Pomodoro Lift directly from boiling pot into pan; never rinse in a colander.
Delicate Stuffed Ravioli Tortelloni The hero is inside the pasta. Heavy sauces drown the filling; simple aromatics complement. Sage & Hazelnut Brown Butter Cook gently on a simmer — a vigorous rolling boil will break the seams.
Fluted & Hollow Campanelle Rigatoni The petals trap chunky vegetables, nuts, sausage bits, and coarse herb pastes. Pesto alla Genovese Dilute cold pesto with warm cooking water before folding into warm pasta.
Pillowy Gnocchi Potato Gnocchi Soft, delicate structure absorbs creamy reductions, melted gorgonzola, and oven-baked cheeses. Truffled Alfredo Crema Remove from water the very second they float to the top to preserve pillowy texture.
